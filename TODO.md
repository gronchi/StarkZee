## 1. Convergence and external validation

Build a reproducible validation matrix before adding another major physics
model. At minimum, vary these independently:

- microfield cutoff and quadrature (`max_beta`, `num_f`);
- angular quadrature (`num_mu`);
- energy-grid spacing and window;
- FFM SDT binning versus the unbinned result;
- static versus FFM results under genuinely matched settings;
- Doppler placement and density-coordinate Jacobians.

Infrastructure is now available in `starkzee.validation` and
`scripts/profile_convergence.py`; its representative static/FFM smoke case
varies the numerical controls above, reports each supported solver Doppler path,
compares static with FFM under matched exposed settings, and reports
area-conservation errors after per-eV densities are transformed to vacuum-nm,
THz, and inverse-cm coordinates. This does not test the alternative ZEST
placement of Doppler inside its construction of J because that operator is not
implemented here and the operations need not commute. This backlog item remains
open until application-specific domains and tolerances are run and an external
reference is added.

Report errors for integrated area, centroid, polarization strength, selected
core/wing intensities, and a norm over the full sampled profile. No fixed
quadrature count or SDT tolerance is a universal safe default. Acceptance needs
declared plasma/transition domains and tolerances, plus at least one external
reference calculation or measurement that was not generated by StarkZee.

The representative report now supplies all of those numerical observables,
including explicitly labeled line-center, core, and wing sample offsets. Their
values remain diagnostics for one smoke case, not acceptance tolerances for an
application domain.

State-resolved natural damping is implemented for the hydrogenic E1 model and
distinguishes the zero E1 rate of 2s from radiating 2p. The 2p, 3s, 3p and 3d
rates now agree within 0.1% with the critically evaluated H I multiplet data
of Wiese and Fuhr (2009), recorded in
``starkzee/data/radiative_benchmarks.json``. This validates the implemented
E1 scope, not total physical lifetimes: two-photon, M1 and other non-E1 decay
channels remain absent. The rates use analytic gross-structure energy gaps
and do not include fine-structure corrections to the radiative rates.

## 2. Inter-shell configuration interaction

The atomic Hamiltonian still diagonalizes one principal shell at a time. This
omits inter-n Stark and diamagnetic coupling and prevents a quantitative claim
to reproduce high-field, high-n results that require configuration interaction.

The first two prerequisites are now implemented in the experimental
``starkzee.multishell`` module. It constructs a globally indexed truncated
multi-n basis, signed inter-shell radial integrals, and Hermitian linear-Stark
and diamagnetic blocks without changing either production profile solver.
``scripts/multishell_ci_reference.py`` varies both shell cutoffs and field
angle for a tracked-state diagnostic. Multi-shell E1 matrices now preserve
total line strength under CI rotation and split a selected target-line window
into target, neighboring-channel, and coherent interference contributions.
The same diagnostic reports their energy-weighted ``gf`` values. Its output is
not yet a stored physical acceptance benchmark.

Five scoped numerical regressions are now recorded in
``starkzee/data/multishell_validation_cases.json``. They sample H-beta at
100, 500, and 1000 T and fixed electric fields from zero through three
Holtsmark normal fields for ``Ne=1e23 m^-3``, at 0, 45, and 90 degrees. The
minimum tested passing upper basis ranges from the target ``n=4`` shell alone
at 100 T and zero electric field to ``n=3..8`` at 1000 T and three normal
fields, using ``1e-5`` eV tracked-energy and 0.1% target/total windowed-``gf``
tolerances. These are finite-reference checks at five sampled points; they are
neither an infinite-basis proof nor a criterion for the continuous microfield
distribution or another transition.

Before integration into the profile solvers, the remaining work is:

1. Extend the declared convergence cases to other intended transitions and
   continuous microfield quadrature. Five H-beta points now cover the sampled
   100--1000 T and zero--three-normal-field boundary set.
2. Establish physical acceptance criteria for oscillator-strength leakage and
   interference from neighboring spectral lines; the accounting machinery is
   implemented, but no universal safe window or truncation has been claimed.
3. Compare against a traceable primary figure/table or reference implementation.
4. Design and validate production static/FFM integration without silently
   double-counting adjacent radiating manifolds.

The earlier exploratory red-shift calculation is not present in this working
tree and therefore is not reproducible evidence. Existing profile examples
remain qualitative demonstrations of the intra-shell production model.

## 3. Full collision and interference operator

The implemented electron-impact operator retains its dressed-state diagonal.
The target for the remaining work is now specified by A. Calisti, S. Ferri,
C. Mossé and B. Talin, *The PPP Code -- User Manual* (2024),
[HAL hal-04501367v1](https://hal.science/hal-04501367v1), especially
Section 2.2, Eqs. (5) and (10)--(13), and Appendix B, Eq. (B1). The local
archival copy is ``scratch/papers/Readme-PPP-2024.pdf``. Although that document
describes PPP rather than the separate Stark--Zeeman interface, it supplies the
collision machinery underlying PPPB much more completely than PPPB Eq. (19)
alone.

Appendix B specifies the optical-coherence-space matrix
``Phi[alpha,alpha',beta,beta']`` as the sum of an upper-manifold term, a
lower-manifold term, and a negative upper--lower interference term. The manual
states that the extra-diagonal pieces of the first two terms are negligible,
whereas the third term couples distinct radiative transitions. The PPP manual's
legacy runtime option ``ric=n`` omits interference and ``ric=y`` retains it;
StarkZee exposes this choice as the descriptive boolean
``electron_interference``. Section 2.2 forms and diagonalizes the non-Hermitian
generator ``L_f - i Phi`` and obtains the
complex generalized frequency ``z_k = omega_k + i gamma_k`` and generalized
intensity ``a_k + i c_k``. The resulting static component contains both the
Lorentzian numerator ``a_k gamma_k`` and the dispersive numerator
``c_k (omega - omega_k)``.

An experimental opt-in implementation is now present in
``starkzee.collision``. ``electron_interference=True`` constructs all three
terms of Appendix B Eq. (B1) in the optical-coherence basis, adds natural
relaxation, diagonalizes ``L_f - i Phi`` with a general complex eigensolver,
and derives ``a_k``, ``c_k``, ``omega_k`` and ``gamma_k`` from the left/right
resolvent residues. The static solver evaluates the manual's generalized
Lorentzian Eq. (12); the FFM retains ``a_k + i c_k`` in its numerator and uses
``|a_k|/sum(|a|)`` for the stationary Markov probabilities. This modulus rule
is a documented StarkZee closure, not a rule recovered from the PPP manual.
The default remains ``electron_interference=False``. The full mode is deliberately limited to the
manual's impact kernel ``G(0)`` and rejects incompatible frequency-dependent
width, SDT-binning and alternate numerical-inversion options.

Internal regressions verify the Appendix-B matrix structure and Hermiticity,
nonnegative damping operator, equality of the complex-SDT decomposition with a
direct non-Hermitian resolvent, conservation of total complex residue, finite
positive Lyman-alpha output, invariance under dressed-state rephasing and
ordering, option validation, and recovery of the full static operator profile
from the FFM at zero fluctuation rate.

The remaining acceptance work is:

1. **Externally blocked:** compare at least one interference-on profile and its component
   ``a_k, c_k, omega_k, gamma_k`` data with PPP/PPPB output or an archived
   reference case before quoting a numerical asymmetry. No matched PPP/PPPB
   numerical component output is present in the workspace; the papers and
   manuals are not substitutes for that dataset.
2. **Model specification required:** determine whether the hydrogenic intermediate-state sum should remain in
   the selected upper/lower shells or use a larger closure, then demonstrate
   basis convergence. The manual defines the sum but does not settle that
   truncation for this implementation. StarkZee therefore uses the declared
   selected upper/lower-shell dipole closure rather than inventing an
   unreferenced larger closure. Its common coefficient is evaluated directly
   as ``W0 [C_nu + G_nu(0)]`` and is no longer calibrated from either scalar
   full-shell or intra-shell width.
3. **Infrastructure implemented; domain acceptance open:** validate regimes where individual real generalized intensities approach
   or cross zero. Structured diagnostics now report negative-mode counts,
   negative absolute-strength fractions, extrema and representative pole
   separations. ``scripts/scan_ffm_interference_residues.py`` maps these over
   configurable transition/plasma/quadrature matrices. The first coarse
   D-alpha/D-gamma survey is recorded in ``notes/FFM_implementation_plan.md``;
   its strong quadrature sensitivity, especially for D-gamma, means this item
   remains open pending higher-order and cutoff convergence over the intended
   application domain.
4. **Kernel specification/reference required:** implement the full detuning-dependent Appendix-B kernel only if needed. It
   requires rebuilding and solving the non-Hermitian generator at each observed
   energy and requires the state-pair-dependent Appendix-B ``G`` arguments and
   cutoff convention, not merely rescaling the existing impact-limit matrix.
   It must not be confused with the existing per-SDT scalar-width sensitivity
   option, and is not implemented without a matched reference case.

## 4. Reference tables and microfield provenance

- The bundled Rosato values are now mapped exhaustively to a pinned 3,000-file
  pystark archive, and the archived reader establishes density, temperature,
  field and detuning units. The original author-to-pystark acquisition, license,
  explicit intensity-unit declaration, numerical uncertainty, and intended
  handling of signed values remain unknown. The current default warns and
  preserves them; clipping is an explicit projection, not a source-data repair.
- The internal Stehle inventory now checks all 84 Lyman/Balmer/Paschen
  transition coordinate sets, active versus padded density nodes, exact-node
  recovery, endpoints, finite-window normalization, reversal invariance, and
  no-extrapolation behavior. The bundled NetCDF is byte-identical to a pinned
  pystark file with the CDS VI/98A raw distribution and conversion script.
  Still verify profile normalization and interpolation against held-out values;
  source traceability and internal self-consistency are not an external benchmark.
- Checksums and available acquisition metadata for every bundled runtime
  reference table are archived in `starkzee/data/reference_tables.json`, with
  unknown fields explicitly retained and a repeatable source verifier.
- If PPPB-equivalent microfields are required, implement or ingest traceable
  APEX/MD tables. The legacy Hooper-like ansatz is not genuine Hooper theory;
  Potekhin is the screened default but is not a substitute for APEX.

A local working collection of PPP, PPPB, ZEST, Calisti, Ferri, Potekhin, and
related sources is present under `scratch/papers`. These convenience copies are
not themselves the archival provenance record; stable DOI/HAL/catalog links and

## 6. Deferred features

Electric-quadrupole emission is not currently justified for the visible/UV
hydrogenic use cases. Reconsider it only for a concrete application with an
estimated observable E2 contribution. Quadrupole coupling to microfield
gradients is a separate high-density extension and must not be presented as E2
emission.
